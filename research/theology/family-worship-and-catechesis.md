# Family Worship and Catechesis — The Home as a Little Church

The practical engine of Reformed/Baptist family discipleship: simple, regular, grace-dependent household worship. This file is deliberately *practical* — it becomes a core chapter in the app (see the [PRD](../../prd/biblical-parenting-guide-app-prd.md)).

## Biblical basis

- **Deuteronomy 6:6–7** — daily, ambient, intentional Word-saturation (full study [here](../scripture-studies/deuteronomy-6-family-discipleship.md)).
- **Psalm 78:5–7 (KJV)** — *"…he commanded our fathers, that they should make them known to their children: That the generation to come might know them… that they might set their hope in God."*
- **Joshua 24:15 (NASB1995)** — *"…as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD."*
- **2 Timothy 1:5; 3:15** — Timothy, taught the sacred writings "from childhood" by mother and grandmother.
- **Job 1:5** — Job rising early to intercede for his children, "continually."

## The historic pattern (Puritan/Baptist heritage)

The Puritans and early Baptists treated the household as a "little church": the father (or believing parent) leading short daily worship — Scripture, prayer, and singing — with catechesis for the children. Spurgeon: the church's health in every age has risen and fallen with family worship. The pattern survives because it is *small, daily, and repeatable* — a whetstone, not a cathedral (Hebrew *shanan*, "teach diligently" = "whet/sharpen").

## A simple, sustainable liturgy (10–15 minutes)

1. **Read** — a short passage (one Proverbs chapter, or a Gospel story). One question: *"What does this show us about God?"*
2. **Sing** — one song or doxology. Toddlers wiggle; sing anyway.
3. **Pray** — short prayers; let children pray; keep a family prayer list (answered prayers are faith fuel — Psalm 78:4).
4. **Catechize** — one question-and-answer, repeated through the week.

**Rules of grace:** Missed a day? Mercy is new tomorrow (Lamentations 3:22–23). Chaos happened? That *was* family worship — you modeled patience. Aim for *consistency over intensity*: five minutes daily beats an hour monthly.

## Catechesis for Baptist families

Historic tools consistent with a Reformed Baptist confession:

- **The Baptist Catechism** (a.k.a. Keach's Catechism, ~1693) — the Baptist adaptation of the Westminster Shorter Catechism. Q1: *"Who is the first and chiefest being? — God is the first and chiefest being."* And the Shorter Catechism's famous opening adapted therein: man's chief end is *"to glorify God, and to enjoy him forever"* (1 Corinthians 10:31; Psalm 73:25–26).
- **A Catechism for Boys and Girls** — simplified first questions for small children (Q1: "Who made you?" — "God made me"; Genesis 1:27).
- **Scripture memory** as the spine: start with Genesis 1:1; Psalm 23; Proverbs 3:5–6; John 3:16; Ephesians 6:1.

Catechesis is *not* rote-ism: it loads the child's memory with truth the Spirit can ignite later (Isaiah 55:11). Always pair Q&A with conversation — *nouthesia* alongside *paideia* (Ephesians 6:4).

## Milestones and moments

| Stage | Emphasis |
|---|---|
| Infancy | Parents' own devotions; praying over the child; singing Scripture; the *parents* are the curriculum |
| Toddler–preschool | First catechism questions; Bible storybooks; table prayers; "God made this" language all day (Deut 6:7) |
| Elementary | Reading Scripture together; memory verses; answering "why" with the gospel (Deut 6:20–25) |
| Pre-teen/teen | Personal devotions coached, not policed; honest doubt welcomed (Jude 22); serving together; conversion and baptism on the child's own profession |
| Release | Arrows loosed (Psalm 127:4); intercession never retires (Job 1:5) |

## Common failure modes (and their gospel answers)

- **Legalism** — worship as family scorekeeping → *return to grace: we worship because we're loved, not to be loved.*
- **Dad-abdication** — Ephesians 6:4 addresses *fathers* first; passivity is the ancient failure (Genesis 3:6, "with her") → *small start beats grand intentions: read one Proverb tonight.*
- **Outsourcing** — church, school, or apps as substitute parents → *they are allies, not replacements (Deut 6:7 is addressed to you).* An AI that accepts the outsourced role violates this repo's values; the skill is designed to hand the work back with encouragement.
- **Performance despair** — comparing your chaotic table to imagined perfect families → *Psalm 127:1–2; God builds; sleep in grace.*

## Heart questions

2. Which failure mode above is closest to our house — and what would grace, not guilt, do about it?
3. Who in our church could we ask to keep us encouraged in this? (Community is a means of grace too.)
