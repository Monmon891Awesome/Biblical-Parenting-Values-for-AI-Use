# Reformed/Baptist Theological Foundations for Parenting

The doctrinal frame for everything in this repository, in the stream of the **Second London Baptist Confession of Faith (1689)** — the historic Reformed Baptist confession shared by figures like John Bunyan's generation and later Charles Spurgeon — read alongside evangelical statements of faith like CCF's (conservative, gospel-centered, Bible-final). Where CCF's broadly evangelical statement and stricter confessional Reformed Baptist positions differ in emphasis, this repo teaches what they hold **in common**: the authority of Scripture, salvation by grace alone through faith alone in Christ alone, and discipleship as the church's mission.

## 1. Sola Scriptura — the sufficient rule for the family

> "The Holy Scripture is the only sufficient, certain, and infallible rule of all saving knowledge, faith, and obedience." — 2LBCF 1.1

**2 Timothy 3:16–17 (NASB1995)** — *"All Scripture is inspired by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, for training in righteousness; so that the man of God may be adequate, equipped for every good work."*

- Parenting advice — books, experts, algorithms, AI — is *ministerial*, never *magisterial*. It serves under Scripture or it is dismissed.
- Practical test this repo bakes into its AI values: every recommendation must be traceable to Biblical text or honestly labeled as prudence, not command.

## 2. The doctrines of grace — and why they liberate parents

Salvation is God's sovereign work from first to last (Ephesians 2:8–9; John 6:44; Romans 9:16). For parents this is not an abstraction; it is oxygen:

| Doctrine | Parenting implication |
|---|---|
| **Total depravity** — children are born sinners (Psalm 51:5; Proverbs 22:15) | We disciple sinners, not blank slates. Behavior modification cannot fix a heart problem; children need the *gospel*, not just guidance. |
| **Unconditional election & effectual calling** — God saves by grace, not pedigree (John 1:12–13) | Godly parenting is a *means* God delights to use, not a mechanism that manufactures faith. This kills both pride ("my methods saved my kids") and despair ("my failures doomed them"). |
| **Particular redemption & perseverance** — Christ actually saves and keeps His people (John 10:28–29; Philippians 1:6) | A believing parent's hope for a wandering child rests on the power of Christ, not the perfection of the childhood. Keep praying: the Shepherd seeks. |

**Philippians 1:6 (NASB1995)** — *"He who began a good work in you will perfect it until the day of Christ Jesus."*

**The pastoral center:** God ordains *means* as well as ends. The Shema's diligence and Proverbs' training are the ordinary means through which God is pleased to call covenant children to Himself. So we labor *diligently* and rest *completely* — Psalm 127's exact combination.

## 3. A credobaptist view of children

Reformed *Baptists* hold that children of believers are **disciples-in-formation under the means of grace**, not presumptively regenerate members of the new covenant:

- Children are brought *to* Christ (Mark 10:13–16) and raised *in* "the discipline and instruction of the Lord" (Ephesians 6:4) — evangelized at home first.
- Parents therefore pray for and look toward the child's own *credible profession of faith*, followed by believer's baptism as the child's own confession (Acts 2:41).
- Practically this means: never assume the lamb is in the fold because it sleeps in the barn. Keep the gospel — not just morals — in front of your children.

## 4. The means of grace in the home

The ordinary means by which God grows His people — **the Word, prayer, and the fellowship of the church** (Acts 2:42) — set the family's rhythm:

1. **Word**: family Bible reading, Scripture memory, catechesis (see [`family-worship-and-catechesis.md`](family-worship-and-catechesis.md)).
2. **Prayer**: with children, for children, as a couple, in secret (Matthew 6:6).
3. **Church**: the gathered body under preaching and the ordinances — not optional for busy families but the household's weekly heartbeat (Hebrews 10:24–25). Small groups / Dgroups extend this into the week.

**Warning this repo encodes for AI:** an app can *point to* the means of grace; it must never *pose as* one. There is no fourth means called "technology."

## 5. God's fatherhood — the pattern and the comfort

- The Father disciplines "for our good, so that we may share His holiness" (Hebrews 12:10, NASB1995) — the template for all correction.
- "Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him. For he knoweth our frame; he remembereth that we are dust" (Psalm 103:13–14, KJV) — the template for all gentleness, including gentleness with *yourself* as a failing parent who is first a beloved child.

## 6. Vocation and the priesthood of believers

Parenting is a *calling* — full-fledged ministry, not a distraction from ministry. Changing diapers, doing devotions, and driving to practice are "good works, which God hath before ordained that we should walk in them" (Ephesians 2:10, KJV). The Reformers recovered this: the home is a little church (Bunyan and the Puritans wrote family-worship manuals for exactly this reason), and father and mother are its first shepherds.

## Summary for the AI value layer

1. Scripture is final; all advice is ministerial.
2. God saves; parents are means. Diligence without messianic pressure.
3. Evangelize your children; assume nothing; pray persistently.
4. Order the home around Word, prayer, and church.
5. Imitate the Father: discipline from love, gentleness that remembers frames of dust.
6. Parenting is vocation — holy work in itself.
