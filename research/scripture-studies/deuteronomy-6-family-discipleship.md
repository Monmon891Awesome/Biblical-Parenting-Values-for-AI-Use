# Deuteronomy 6:4–9 — The Shema: God's Blueprint for Family Discipleship

The oldest and deepest parenting text in Scripture. Jesus called verse 5 "the great and foremost commandment" (Matthew 22:37–38).

## The text in parallel

**Deuteronomy 6:4–7 (KJV)**
> Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD: And thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart, and with all thy soul, and with all thy might. And these words, which I command thee this day, shall be in thine heart: And thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children, and shalt talk of them when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up.

**Deuteronomy 6:4–7 (NASB1995)**
> Hear, O Israel! The LORD is our God, the LORD is one! You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your might. These words, which I am commanding you today, shall be on your heart. You shall teach them diligently to your sons and shall talk of them when you sit in your house and when you walk by the way and when you lie down and when you rise up.

Verses 8–9 extend the saturation: bind them on the hand, between the eyes, write them on doorposts and gates — God's words attached to *work* (hand), *perception* (eyes), *home* (doorposts), and *public life* (gates).

## Word-origin keys (full studies in [`../word-studies/hebrew-word-studies.md`](../word-studies/hebrew-word-studies.md))

| Word | Insight |
|---|---|
| *Shema* (hear) | Hearing and obeying are one verb in Hebrew — v.4 |
| *Ahav* (love) | Commanded covenant allegiance of the whole person — v.5 |
| *Levav* (heart) | Mind + will + affections; the words go *on the parent's heart first* — v.6 |
| *Shanan* (teach diligently) | Lit. "whet/sharpen" — repeated strokes, not one lecture — v.7 |
| *Derek* (way) | Discipleship happens while *walking*, in ordinary life — v.7 |

## The order is the message

1. **v.4 — Theology first.** Who God is comes before anything about family. Parenting is downstream of worship.
2. **v.5 — The parent's love for God.** Before children are ever mentioned, the parent is commanded to love God totally.
3. **v.6 — The parent's own heart.** "These words… shall be *on your heart*." You cannot teach diligently what you do not treasure personally. This is Tan-Chi's Modeling and the reason the AI skill always asks about the parent's own walk before dispensing technique.
4. **v.7 — Then the children.** Formation is *diligent* (intentional) and *ambient* (sitting, walking, lying down, rising — the four corners of an ordinary day).

## Principles

- **Parents are the primary disciplers.** Not the school, not the church program, not media, not AI. Others assist; parents own it (cf. Psalm 78:5–7; Ephesians 6:4).
- **Discipleship is scheduled *and* spontaneous.** The Shema names ordinary moments — meals, commutes, bedtime, mornings — as the classroom.
- **Atmosphere over event.** Doorposts and gates (vv.8–9): the *environment* preaches. Today that includes what's on our phones and what our children see us reach for when we're tired.
- **Deuteronomy 6:20–25 anticipates questions.** "When thy son asketh thee in time to come, saying, What mean the testimonies…?" (v.20, KJV) — parents should *welcome* hard questions and answer with the gospel story (vv.21–25). An AI aligned with this repo does the same: it treats a user's question as a doorway to Scripture, never as a transaction to close.

## Heart questions

1. Is God's Word on *my* heart this week — or am I trying to hand my children something I'm not holding?
2. Which of the four Shema moments (sit / walk / lie down / rise up) is emptiest of God-talk in our home right now?
3. What do the "doorposts" of our house — screens included — say we love?
