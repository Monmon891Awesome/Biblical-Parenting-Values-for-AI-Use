# Proverbs on Parenting — Wisdom, Discipline, and the Heart

Proverbs is covenant wisdom: inspired generalizations about how God's world ordinarily works, addressed "my son" from a father who wants his child to *fear the LORD* (Proverbs 1:7). It is not a vending machine of guarantees — and reading it that way crushes parents and misreads the genre (see [`../00-methodology.md`](../00-methodology.md)).

## 1. The foundation: the fear of the LORD

**Proverbs 1:7 (KJV)** — *"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge: but fools despise wisdom and instruction."*
**(NASB1995)** — *"The fear of the LORD is the beginning of knowledge; Fools despise wisdom and instruction."*

Parenting aims at *reverent relationship with God*, not merely educated, well-behaved children.

## 2. Train up — dedication and direction

**Proverbs 22:6 (KJV)** — *"Train up a child in the way he should go: and when he is old, he will not depart from it."*
**(NASB1995)** — *"Train up a child in the way he should go, Even when he is old he will not depart from it."*

- *Chanak* = train/dedicate (word study [here](../word-studies/hebrew-word-studies.md#1-חנך--chanak-strongs-h2596--train-up--dedicate)); parenting is consecration.
- A wisdom generalization, not a mechanical promise: faithful training ordinarily bears fruit, while salvation remains God's sovereign gift (see [`../theology/reformed-baptist-foundations.md`](../theology/reformed-baptist-foundations.md)). This truth both *motivates* diligence and *frees* parents from taking credit or final blame for a child's heart.

## 3. Instruction from father AND mother

**Proverbs 1:8–9 (NASB1995)** — *"Hear, my son, your father's instruction And do not forsake your mother's teaching; Indeed, they are a graceful wreath to your head And ornaments about your neck."*
**(KJV)** — *"My son, hear the instruction of thy father, and forsake not the law of thy mother: for they shall be an ornament of grace unto thy head, and chains about thy neck."*

Both parents teach; their combined instruction *adorns* a child. Discipline done right makes a child more glorious, not more crushed.

## 4. Discipline as love

**Proverbs 3:11–12 (KJV)** — *"My son, despise not the chastening of the LORD; neither be weary of his correction: For whom the LORD loveth he correcteth; even as a father the son in whom he delighteth."*

**Proverbs 13:24 (NASB1995)** — *"He who withholds his rod hates his son, But he who loves him disciplines him diligently."*

**Proverbs 29:15, 17 (KJV)** — *"The rod and reproof give wisdom: but a child left to himself bringeth his mother to shame… Correct thy son, and he shall give thee rest; yea, he shall give delight unto thy soul."*

- The model is *God's* fatherhood — correction flows from delight (3:12), never from rage. Hebrews 12:5–11 quotes this passage as the charter of all discipline.
- "Rod and *reproof*" (29:15): consequence **and** conversation together — the OT twin of *paideia* and *nouthesia* in Ephesians 6:4.
- The boundary is absolute: discipline that provokes, shames, or breaks a child's spirit is forbidden by the same Bible (Colossians 3:21). Where faithful Christians differ on specific practices, this repo teaches the *principles* — love-motivated, self-controlled, restorative correction — and sends parents to Scripture, their church elders, and the laws of their land for the particulars.

## 5. The heart is the target

**Proverbs 4:23 (NASB1995)** — *"Watch over your heart with all diligence, For from it flow the springs of life."*

**Proverbs 22:15 (NASB1995)** — *"Foolishness is bound up in the heart of a child; The rod of discipline will remove it far from him."*

Foolishness is a *heart* condition, so correction must reach the heart. "The heart is the root; behaviour is the fruit" (Peter Tan-Chi). Techniques that only rearrange behavior leave the root untouched.

## 6. Words that give life

**Proverbs 15:1 (KJV)** — *"A soft answer turneth away wrath: but grievous words stir up anger."*
**Proverbs 18:21 (NASB1995)** — *"Death and life are in the power of the tongue."*
**Proverbs 16:24 (KJV)** — *"Pleasant words are as an honeycomb, sweet to the soul, and health to the bones."*

The home's vocabulary either builds or breaks. This is the research basis for the skill's insistence on *affirmation* (the "A" in MOTIVATE) and gentle speech in every generated response.

## 7. The parent's own dependence

**Proverbs 3:5–6 (NASB1995)** — *"Trust in the LORD with all your heart And do not lean on your own understanding. In all your ways acknowledge Him, And He will make your paths straight."*

The parenting book of the Bible tells parents not to lean on their *own* understanding — how much less on a machine's. Wisdom is asked for and given by God (James 1:5), not downloaded.

## Principles

1. Aim at the fear of the LORD, not performance.
2. Train = dedicate: consecrate the child and the process to God.
3. Discipline is love in action — from delight, with self-control, rod-and-reproof together, restorative in goal.
4. Target the heart; behavior follows.
5. Guard the tongue; words build or destroy.
6. Lean on the LORD, not on your own understanding — or anyone else's substitute for Him.

## Heart questions

1. When I correct my child, what is my face saying about my heart?
2. Is my discipline *teaching wisdom* or just *stopping noise*?
3. What did my child hear me say about them this week — to them and about them?
