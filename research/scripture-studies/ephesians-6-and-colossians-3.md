# Ephesians 6:1–4 & Colossians 3:20–21 — The Household Codes in Christ

Paul's two "household codes" give the New Testament's most direct parenting commands. Both sit inside letters about union with Christ — parenting instructions are *applications of the gospel*, not standalone rules.

## The text in parallel

**Ephesians 6:1–4 (KJV)**
> Children, obey your parents in the Lord: for this is right. Honour thy father and mother; (which is the first commandment with promise;) That it may be well with thee, and thou mayest live long on the earth. And, ye fathers, provoke not your children to wrath: but bring them up in the nurture and admonition of the Lord.

**Ephesians 6:1–4 (NASB1995)**
> Children, obey your parents in the Lord, for this is right. Honor your father and mother (which is the first commandment with a promise), so that it may be well with you, and that you may live long on the earth. Fathers, do not provoke your children to anger, but bring them up in the discipline and instruction of the Lord.

**Colossians 3:20–21 (KJV)**
> Children, obey your parents in all things: for this is well pleasing unto the Lord. Fathers, provoke not your children to anger, lest they be discouraged.

**Colossians 3:20–21 (NASB1995)**
> Children, be obedient to your parents in all things, for this is well-pleasing to the Lord. Fathers, do not exasperate your children, so that they will not lose heart.

| Eph 6:4 / Col 3:21 | "provoke" | "provoke / exasperate" | *parorgizō* / *erethizō* | [link](../word-studies/greek-word-studies.md#4-παροργίζω--parorgizō-g3949-and-ἐρεθίζω--erethizō-g2042--provoke-exasperate) |

The two translations *together* give the full picture: KJV's "nurture" preserves *paideia*'s warmth; NASB's "discipline" preserves its structure. This is the parallel-reading method paying off.

## Observations

1. **Children are addressed directly** (6:1) — they are moral agents in the congregation, disciples in formation, expected to hear the Word read. Christian parenting talks *with* children about God, not merely *about* children.
2. **Obedience is "in the Lord."** The child's obedience is discipleship, grounded in the fifth commandment (Exodus 20:12) — the first commandment with a promise attached.
3. **The command to fathers is a restraint on power.** In a culture of near-absolute paternal authority (*patria potestas*), the Spirit's first word to fathers is *do not provoke*. Authority in Christ's household exists to nourish, never to crush.
4. **Colossians adds the diagnosis:** exasperated children "lose heart" (*athymeō*). Signs include discouragement, secrecy, giving up trying. The child's *spirit* is a gauge parents must watch.
5. **"Of the Lord"** (6:4) — the training belongs to Jesus. Parents are under-shepherds of children who ultimately belong to God. So is any tool the parents use, AI included: it operates inside "the discipline and instruction *of the Lord*" or it has no business in the nursery.

## Common ways parents provoke (drawn from the text's logic and pastoral usage)

Inconsistency; hypocrisy (Modeling failure); harsh or mocking words (contra Proverbs 15:1); favoritism (Genesis 37:3–4's warning); impossible standards; discipline in rage; broken promises; absence — physical or attentional. *(Attentional absence includes the parent lost in a screen. An app built from this repo must never make that worse — see the session-boundary guardrail in [`../../ai-values/guardrails.md`](../../ai-values/guardrails.md).)*

## Principles

1. Nourish (*ektrephō*): parent the way Christ tends His church — feeding growth, not forcing output.
2. Use both channels: structured training (*paideia*) and reasoned, heart-reaching words (*nouthesia*).
3. Guard the child's spirit: the prohibition on provoking is as binding as the command to train.
4. Keep it "of the Lord": Christ sets the curriculum, the tone, and the goal.

## Heart questions

1. Which channel do I overuse — consequences without conversation, or talk without follow-through?
2. Is there any sign my child is "losing heart" around me?
3. If my child becomes my kind of disciple (Luke 6:40), what exactly will they become?
