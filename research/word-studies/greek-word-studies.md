# Greek Word Studies — Bringing Up Children in the Lord (New Testament)

Method: see [`../00-methodology.md`](../00-methodology.md). Glosses follow Strong's/Thayer (public domain), checked against standard usage.

---

## 1. παιδεία — *paideia* (Strong's G3809) — "discipline, training, nurture"

**Key text:** Ephesians 6:4 — KJV *"bring them up in the nurture… of the Lord"*; NASB1995 *"bring them up in the discipline… of the Lord."*

- **Origin:** From *pais* ("child"). In Greek culture *paideia* was the entire process of raising a child into maturity — education, culture, formation — not just punishment.
- **Usage:** Hebrews 12:5–11 uses *paideia* to translate Hebrew *musar* (via Proverbs 3:11–12 LXX), teaching that God disciplines every child He loves; "afterwards it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness" (Hebrews 12:11, NASB1995). 2 Timothy 3:16 — Scripture itself is profitable "for training [*paideia*] in righteousness."
- **Payoff:** *Paideia tou Kyriou* — "the discipline of the LORD" — means the Lord is both the *curriculum* and the *teacher*. Parents administer a formation whose content and manner belong to Christ.

## 2. νουθεσία — *nouthesia* (G3559) — "admonition, instruction"

**Key text:** Ephesians 6:4 — KJV *"admonition of the Lord"*; NASB1995 *"instruction of the Lord."*

- **Origin:** From *nous* ("mind") + *tithēmi* ("to place") — literally *placing in the mind*: reasoned, verbal, corrective counsel.
- **Usage:** The verb *noutheteō* appears in Acts 20:31 (Paul warning the Ephesian elders "with tears"), Colossians 3:16 ("admonishing one another"), 1 Thessalonians 5:14.
- **Payoff:** Where *paideia* shapes by training and consequence, *nouthesia* shapes by words that reach the mind — appeal, warning, explanation. Ephesians 6:4 commands **both**: structured formation *and* heart-level conversation.

## 3. ἐκτρέφω — *ektrephō* (G1625) — "nourish, bring up"

**Key text:** Ephesians 6:4 — KJV *"bring them up"*; NASB1995 *"bring them up."*

- **Origin:** *ek* ("out of") + *trephō* ("to nourish, feed") — to rear by feeding, as a plant is grown.
- **Usage:** Paul uses the same verb one chapter earlier of Christ nourishing the church (Ephesians 5:29). Fathers nourish children the way Christ nourishes His body.
- **Payoff:** Parenting is *feeding*, not manufacturing. You cultivate conditions for growth; God gives the increase (1 Corinthians 3:6–7).

## 4. παροργίζω — *parorgizō* (G3949) and ἐρεθίζω — *erethizō* (G2042) — "provoke, exasperate"

**Key texts:** Ephesians 6:4 — KJV *"provoke not your children to wrath"*; NASB1995 *"do not provoke your children to anger."* Colossians 3:21 — KJV *"provoke not your children to anger, lest they be discouraged"*; NASB1995 *"do not exasperate your children, so that they will not lose heart."*

- **Origin:** *parorgizō*: *para* (beside/toward) + *orgizō* (to anger) — to push someone into settled anger. *erethizō*: to stir up, irritate, rub raw.
- **Usage note:** Colossians adds the outcome — *athymeō*, "to lose heart, be dispirited" (from *a-* + *thymos*, spirit/passion): a child whose spirit has been deflated.
- **Payoff:** The only *negative* command to fathers in both household codes targets the *parent's* sin: harshness, inconsistency, favoritism, impossible standards, hypocrisy. Biblical authority is bounded by the child's heart-health. This grounds the wellbeing and gentleness values in [`../../ai-values/VALUES.md`](../../ai-values/VALUES.md).

## 5. ἀγάπη — *agapē* (G26) — "love"

**Key texts:** 1 Corinthians 13:4–7; John 13:34–35; 1 John 4:19.

- **Usage:** Self-giving, covenantal love defined by God's own act: "We love, because He first loved us" (1 John 4:19, NASB1995). 1 Corinthians 13:4 — "Charity suffereth long, and is kind" (KJV); "Love is patient, love is kind" (NASB1995).
- **Payoff:** *Agapē* is the household's operating system. Discipline without *agapē* is provocation (see #4); affection without truth is sentimentality. The AI value "Truth in love" (Ephesians 4:15) comes straight from this word.

## 6. μαθητής — *mathētēs* (G3101) — "disciple, learner"

**Key texts:** Matthew 28:19–20 (the Great Commission — CCF's namesake); Luke 6:40.

- **Origin:** From *manthanō*, "to learn" — a disciple is an apprentice-learner attached to a master.
- **Usage:** "A pupil is not above his teacher; but everyone, after he has been fully trained, will be like his teacher" (Luke 6:40, NASB1995).
- **Payoff:** Children become what their teachers *are*, not what their teachers *say*. Hence Modeling is first in MOTIVATE and MRI. And an AI must never accept the "master" seat: Christ is the Master; the AI at best hands the user back to Him.

## 7. οἶκος — *oikos* (G3624) — "house, household"

**Key texts:** Acts 16:31–34; Joshua 24:15 (LXX); 2 Timothy 1:5; 3:15.

- **Usage:** The NT household is a discipleship unit — the jailer believes "with all his house" (Acts 16:34); Timothy's faith lived first in grandmother Lois and mother Eunice (2 Timothy 1:5), who taught him "the sacred writings" from infancy (2 Timothy 3:15, NASB1995: "from childhood you have known the sacred writings").
- **Payoff:** God's ordinary strategy for the gospel runs through households. "As for me and my house, we will serve the LORD" (Joshua 24:15).

## 8. μεταμορφόω — *metamorphoō* (G3339) — "transform" and ἀνακαίνωσις — *anakainōsis* (G342) — "renewing"

**Key text:** Romans 12:2 — KJV *"be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind"*; NASB1995 *"be transformed by the renewing of your mind."*

- **Origin:** *metamorphoō* — change of form from the inside out (used of the Transfiguration, Matthew 17:2). *anakainōsis* — making new again.
- **Payoff:** **This is the anti-AI-dependence word.** Renewal happens in the *user's own mind* engaging God's Word by the Spirit. An AI that thinks *for* a Christian blocks the very faculty God intends to renew. The skill therefore asks questions, assigns the reading, and gets out of the way ([`../../ai-values/guardrails.md`](../../ai-values/guardrails.md)).

## 9. ἀνάπαυσις — *anapausis* (G372) — "rest"

**Key text:** Matthew 11:28–29 — KJV *"Come unto me… and I will give you rest… ye shall find rest unto your souls"*; NASB1995 *"Come to Me, all who are weary and heavy-laden, and I will give you rest… you will find rest for your souls."*

- **Origin:** *ana* (again) + *pauō* (to cease) — cessation that restores.
- **Payoff:** Rest is a Person's gift, not a productivity technique. The parental-wellbeing track and the skill's "weary parent" pathway both terminate here — at Christ, not at coping tips.

## 10. παράκλητος — *paraklētos* (G3875) — "Helper, Comforter, Advocate"

**Key texts:** John 14:16–17, 26; 16:13.

- **Origin:** *para* (alongside) + *kaleō* (to call) — one called alongside to help.
- **Usage:** Jesus names the Holy Spirit "another Helper… the Spirit of truth" who "will teach you all things" (John 14:16, 26, NASB1995).
- **Payoff:** **The Helper's chair is taken.** Any technology that markets itself as the believer's ever-present counselor competes for a title Scripture gives to the Holy Spirit alone. The correct posture for AI: a concordance with a kind voice — useful, limited, and always deferring to the true Paraclete.
