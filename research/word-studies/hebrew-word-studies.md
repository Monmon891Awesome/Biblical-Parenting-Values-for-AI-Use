# Hebrew Word Studies — Parenting, Training, and the Heart of the Old Testament Family

Method: see [`../00-methodology.md`](../00-methodology.md). Glosses follow Strong's/BDB (public domain), checked against standard usage. Etymology *suggests*; Biblical usage *decides*.

---

## 1. חָנַךְ — *chanak* (Strong's H2596) — "train up / dedicate"

**Key text:** Proverbs 22:6 — KJV *"Train up a child in the way he should go"*; NASB1995 *"Train up a child in the way he should go."*

- **Origin:** The root idea is *to initiate, to dedicate, to inaugurate*. It is used of dedicating a house (Deuteronomy 20:5) and the temple (1 Kings 8:63). The related noun *chanukkah* ("dedication") names the feast of Hanukkah (cf. John 10:22).
- **Payoff:** "Training" a child is not mere behavior management; it is *dedication* — setting a child apart toward God from the beginning, the way a house is dedicated before anyone lives in it. Parenting is an act of consecration before it is an act of correction.
- **Note on "in the way he should go" (עַל־פִּי דַרְכּוֹ, *al-pi darko*, lit. "according to his way"):** faithful interpreters read this either as (a) the way of wisdom he *ought* to take, or (b) training suited to the child's stage/bent. Both readings are grammatically possible; both are pastorally useful; the verse remains a wisdom generalization, not a mechanical guarantee.

## 2. מוּסָר — *musar* (H4148) and יָסַר — *yasar* (H3256) — "discipline, instruction, correction"

**Key texts:** Proverbs 1:8; 3:11–12; 13:24; 22:15; 23:13.

- **Origin:** *Musar* is the noun of *yasar*, "to discipline, chasten, instruct." It covers the whole spectrum from verbal instruction to corrective consequence.
- **Usage:** Proverbs 1:8 — KJV *"My son, hear the instruction [musar] of thy father"*; NASB1995 *"Hear, my son, your father's instruction."* Proverbs 3:11–12 grounds all discipline in fatherly love — quoted in Hebrews 12:5–6 with Greek *paideia* (see the Greek study), welding the two Testaments together.
- **Payoff:** Biblical discipline is *formative before it is punitive*. Its goal is wisdom in the child, and its model is God the Father, "for whom the LORD loveth he correcteth; even as a father the son in whom he delighteth" (Proverbs 3:12, KJV).

## 3. שָׁמַע — *shama* (H8085) — "hear / obey"

**Key text:** Deuteronomy 6:4 — KJV *"Hear, O Israel"*; NASB1995 *"Hear, O Israel!"* — the *Shema*.

- **Origin & usage:** *Shama* means both "hear" and "obey" — Hebrew has no separate everyday word for "obey"; to truly hear God *is* to respond. Children "hearing" instruction (Proverbs 1:8) and Israel "hearing" the LORD are the same verb.
- **Payoff:** Obedience in the home is discipleship of the ear before it is compliance of the hands. Parents model *shama* by being visibly under God's Word themselves.

## 4. שָׁנַן — *shanan* (H8150) — "teach diligently"

**Key text:** Deuteronomy 6:7 — KJV *"thou shalt teach them diligently unto thy children"*; NASB1995 *"You shall teach them diligently to your sons."*

- **Origin:** The root means *to sharpen, to whet* (used of arrows and swords, e.g., Psalm 45:5; Deuteronomy 32:41). The Piel intensive form in Deuteronomy 6:7 pictures repeated strokes on a whetstone.
- **Payoff:** Teaching children God's words is not a single lecture but a *thousand repeated strokes* — at home, on the road, at bedtime, at breakfast (Deuteronomy 6:7). Formation is rhythmic and relational, exactly what CCF's discipleship model calls *intentionality* (see [`../ccf/ccf-values-and-teachings.md`](../ccf/ccf-values-and-teachings.md)).

## 5. תּוֹרָה — *torah* (H8451) — "law, instruction"

**Key texts:** Proverbs 1:8 ("the law [torah] of thy mother"); Psalm 78:1–7.

- **Origin:** From *yarah* (H3384), "to throw, shoot, point, direct" — the same root behind *moreh*, "teacher." *Torah* is fundamentally *direction pointed out*, not bare legislation.
- **Payoff:** A mother's *torah* (Proverbs 1:8) is trajectory-setting guidance. Psalm 78:5–7 makes intergenerational transmission its explicit purpose: "that the generation to come might know them… that they might set their hope in God" (KJV).

## 6. אָהַב — *ahav* (H157) — "love"

**Key text:** Deuteronomy 6:5 — KJV *"thou shalt love the LORD thy God with all thine heart"*; NASB1995 *"You shall love the LORD your God with all your heart."*

- **Usage:** Covenant love that is commanded — therefore not first a feeling but a whole-person allegiance (heart, soul, might) that *produces* affection. Jesus calls this the first and greatest commandment (Matthew 22:37–38).
- **Payoff:** The Shema commands parents to love God *before* it commands them to teach children (6:5 → 6:7). You cannot impart what you do not possess. This is the "M" (Modeling) of Tan-Chi's MOTIVATE and the "M" of MRI.

## 7. חֶסֶד — *hesed* (H2617) — "lovingkindness, steadfast covenant love"

**Key texts:** Lamentations 3:22–23; Psalm 103:13,17; Exodus 34:6–7.

- **Origin & usage:** Loyal love inside a covenant relationship — love that keeps showing up. NASB1995: "The LORD's lovingkindnesses indeed never cease, For His compassions never fail. They are new every morning" (Lamentations 3:22–23). Psalm 103:13: "Like as a father pitieth his children, so the LORD pitieth them that fear him" (KJV).
- **Payoff:** *Hesed* is the emotional climate of a Biblical home: dependable, promise-keeping love. For weary parents, God's *hesed* is "new every morning" — the anchor text of [`../parenting/parental-wellbeing.md`](../parenting/parental-wellbeing.md).

## 8. בֵּן / בָּנִים — *ben / banim* (H1121) — "son(s), children" and בָּנָה — *banah* (H1129) — "to build"

**Key text:** Psalm 127:1, 3.

- **Origin:** Hebrew ears heard *banim* ("children") next to *banah* ("build") — Psalm 127 exploits the wordplay: "Except the LORD build [banah] the house…" (v.1) … "children [banim] are an heritage of the LORD" (v.3, KJV).
- **Payoff:** Children are how God *builds* a house. And unless *He* builds, the builders labor in vain — the definitive verse against parenting-by-self-effort, and against outsourcing formation to any tool, AI included.

## 9. דֶּרֶךְ — *derek* (H1870) — "way, road"

**Key texts:** Proverbs 22:6; Deuteronomy 6:7 ("when thou walkest by the way"); Psalm 1:1,6.

- **Usage:** The dominant OT metaphor for a life course — the way of the righteous vs. the way of the wicked (Psalm 1:6).
- **Payoff:** Parenting is orientation onto a road, walked *together* — instruction happens "by the way" (Deuteronomy 6:7), in ordinary motion, not only in formal lessons.

## 10. לֵבָב / לֵב — *levav / lev* (H3824/H3820) — "heart"

**Key texts:** Deuteronomy 6:5–6; Proverbs 4:23.

- **Usage:** In Hebrew anthropology the heart is the control center — mind, will, and affections together, not merely emotion. "Keep thy heart with all diligence; for out of it are the issues of life" (Proverbs 4:23, KJV); NASB1995: "Watch over your heart with all diligence, For from it flow the springs of life."
- **Payoff:** "The heart is the root; behaviour is the fruit" (Peter Tan-Chi). All Biblical parenting — and every value in [`../../ai-values/VALUES.md`](../../ai-values/VALUES.md) — targets the heart, not surface compliance.

## 11. קָוָה — *qavah* (H6960) — "to wait for, hope in"

**Key text:** Isaiah 40:31 — NASB1995 *"Yet those who wait for the LORD Will gain new strength"*; KJV *"they that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength."*

- **Origin:** Root idea of a cord under tension — hope stretched toward God.
- **Payoff:** The exhausted parent's word. Strength is *renewed by waiting on God* — in prayer and the Word — not by one more scroll, one more search, one more AI answer.
